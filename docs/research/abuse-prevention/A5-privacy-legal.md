# A5 — Privacy & legal compliance audit of DESIGN-v0

**Audit target:** `DESIGN-v0.md` (device identity for EasyQuran), read against `R2-legal-privacy.md` (research report, October 2026) and the repo as it actually exists.
**Auditor:** privacy/legal compliance subagent. **Date:** 12 October 2026.
**Method:** layer-by-layer hook analysis; primary sources as fetched/cited in R2; repo inspection of the actual privacy surface (`web/`, `rust/backend/api/`, `docs/`).

**Not legal advice.** R2's uncertainty register applies in full. The most load-bearing unverified items for this audit: EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 is still v1.0 draft; the DUAA Schedule 4 "recognised legitimate interests" list was not read; the AI Act dates come from a third-party mirror; WP248/WP251 wording was not fetched; DPDP s.7 "legitimate uses" was not verified; the DSA Art 19 structural reading (Art 17 not exempted) is R2's analysis, not a quoted regulator statement.

**Scope note:** the design is a _security-only_ system. Where this audit says "consent is required", it means the ePrivacy/PECR terminal-access layer or the Indian processing layer, not the GDPR processing layer — those are different questions (R2 §0) and the design conflates them.

## 2. Retention ladder audit: raw → 30d key → 12mo ban → aggregates

### 2.1 "Raw signals never stored"

Correct as a rule (Art 5(1)(c), R2 §3.4(1)) — though `user_sessions` already persists raw UA + IP per login (A8 F6). **But there are two backdoors as written:** `evidence JSON` on `device_records` and the reuse of `audit_logs` for evidence. If a human reviewer needs "the raw signals available" (R2 §7.1 recommendation) and the design stores them in `evidence`, raw signals _are_ stored and the ladder collapses. **Requirement:** evidence = decision metadata only; if raw signals are needed for review they live in a ≤72h ephemeral store with its own expiry, are never written to `audit_logs`, and are deleted after the review window.

### 2.2 Is 30 days for the keyed identifier defensible?

**Yes, conditionally — and not as written.**

- There is no DPA safe harbour (R2 §3.1). 30d matches the vendor norm (Fingerprint.com "typical 30 days") and is short enough to be unremarkable. Defensible **for EU/UK** if: (i) tied to the stated purpose (recognising the device for auth + ban-evasion detection); (ii) enforced by `expires_at` + a deletion job; (iii) never joined to content; (iv) documented in the retention schedule with the necessity argument (CNIL: "il appartient alors au responsable du fichier de la déterminer … sous réserve de documenter son choix").
- **Defect A — no `expires_at` in m000012.** The ladder is a sentence, not a mechanism. Art 5(1)(e) violations are found in the schema, not the policy. Add `expires_at NOT NULL` + CHECK + job (R2 §8.5).
- **Defect B — sliding window.** `last_seen` updates on every sighting. If retention is "30d from last_seen", an active device's record never expires. That is defensible _while the device is in active use_ (the purpose is live), but it must be stated as such; an orphan record (no account, no ban) must have a hard outer cap (recommend: 30d inactivity for orphans, and a documented cap for actives).
- **Defect C — India.** DPDP Rules 2025 Rule 8(3) requires a Data Fiduciary to retain personal data + "associated traffic data and other logs of the processing" for a **minimum of one year** for the prescribed purposes, then erase. 30d may under-retain where Indian law requires retention — and the correct resolution is **not** to extend to 1 year, it is to not process Indian device data at all without consent (§3(d)). Flag both directions in the retention schedule.
- Not defensible in India without consent; not an issue in the US; fine in the UK under Sch A1.

### 2.3 Do coherence booleans + trust_state + seen_count constitute a fingerprint?

**Yes — in effect, and the answer needs to be precise because the design's "not behavioral biometrics" claim leans on the word "fingerprint".**

- Legally: the row is **pseudonymous personal data** (Art 4(1), Art 4(5); EDPB Guidelines 01/2025; Recital 30 names "online identifiers … factors specific to the … device" as identification data). It is **keyed by a fingerprint-derived key** (`UNIQUE(device_key)`), so the row _is_ a device identifier with extra columns.
- The three fields add a **behavioural profile of the device**: how often it is seen (`seen_count`, `last_seen`), how consistent its signals are (`coherence booleans`), and a trust judgement (`trust_state`). GDPR Art 4(4) "profiling" is any automated processing to evaluate personal aspects — device trust scoring evaluates reliability/behaviour of the person associated with the device. LGPD Art 12 §2 makes the same point explicitly ("data used to form a behavioural profile of an identified person can still be personal data"). CCPA: a "unique identifier … or other forms of persistent or probabilistic identifiers" (§1798.140(aj)) — PI, not SPI.
- It is **not** biometric data (Art 4(14)): the fields derive from device configuration and sighting history, not from a person's physical/physiological/behavioural characteristics. See §4 for the line.
- **Consequence:** the booleans/counters must be covered by the same expiry as the key. If the ban record retains `trust_state`, `seen_count`, or `coherence booleans` beyond the key's life, the retention ladder leaks and the "ban record = key + reason + date + reviewer" claim (R2 §9.5) is false. **On key expiry: delete the row or reduce it to a count-only aggregate with no key and no profile fields.**

### 2.4 Does the ladder hold while a ban is active? (evidence freeze)

The design has no freeze rule at all. The correct model:

- **Ban record** (device_key + reason class + ban window + decision ref + expiry): **12 months** — the ban's purpose (prevent re-entry) is live for its duration + appeal window; 12mo is also the shortest period that catches seasonal/repeat abuse (R2 §3.4(3)), and it satisfies India Rule 8(3)'s 1-year floor for any Indian record that lawfully exists.
- **Legal basis for longer retention for banned users:** **Art 6(1)(f)** (defence of legal claims; Recital 47 fraud) + **Art 17(3)(e)** (restriction of erasure for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims) + Art 6(1)(c) where a legal obligation applies (LE request, regulator inquiry). Note Art 17(3)(e) is a _restriction_ mechanism, not a general extension licence.
- **The required change:** there must be **no category rule** ("banned users get longer retention"). There must be a **per-matter legal hold**: a row/flag with `hold_reason`, `hold_authority` (claim/LE/regulator ref), `hold_owner`, `hold_expires_at`, and a documented release. Evidence for a ban that is not under hold expires with the 12mo record. This is the difference between an Art 5(1)(e) finding and a defensible retention decision.
- Also: the ban record must survive account deletion (that is why `user_id SET NULL` exists) — that is lawful under Art 6(1)(f)/17(3)(e) but must be disclosed in the policy and in the erasure response (§5).

## 3. Consent UI by jurisdiction — definitive answers

The design's "no consent UI" is a single global answer to a question that has four different answers. Verdict per jurisdiction:

### (a) EU — **consent required for the client-probe set as designed; not required if collection moves server-side**

- **Terminal access (ePrivacy Art 5(3)):** fingerprinting is in scope (EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2.0 ¶2, quoting WP29 Opinion 9/2014; Planet49 C-673/17 ¶70: "any information stored in such terminal equipment, regardless of whether or not it is personal data"). The exemption is "strictly necessary in order for the provider of an information society service explicitly requested by the subscriber or user to provide the service". **The EDPB deliberately refuses to interpret it** — ¶4: "these circumstances should be analysed on a case-by-case basis accounting for the relevant member state transposition(s), and guidance issued by national Competent Authorities." So there is no EU-wide security exemption to lean on for a high-entropy client-side probe set.
- **Definitive:** if the design keeps the L1 client collector as written (WebGL/WebGPU renderer + extension hash + shader precision, WASM/JS timing, UA-CH high-entropy, platform/feature matrix), **you need consent in the EU** — just-in-time, purpose-specific, at the login/signup/report action, Accept and Decline of equal weight, one click, reader never gated (R2 §4.3). If the design moves the fingerprint to server-side headers/TLS only (R2 §8.4), the strictly-necessary argument becomes citable and **no consent is required**, provided the per-Member-State analysis is written down.
- **GDPR layer:** Art 6(1)(f) is the basis; **do not use consent here** — it would let a banned user withdraw the basis for the ban record.
- **Minimum lawful implementation:** rewritten policy + one-page device-security addendum + LIA + DPIA + per-MS ePrivacy analysis; plus the just-in-time panel **only if** client probes stay. No banner on the reader. CNIL Sheet n°15: one tracer, one purpose — and the design must never reuse the key.

### (b) UK — **no consent required, including the client probes**

- PECR Schedule A1 ¶4(2) (inserted by DUAA 2025 s.112, in force 5 Feb 2026, S.I. 2026/82) makes storage/access strictly necessary if it is, for example, "to ensure that the security of the terminal equipment … is not adversely affected", "to prevent or detect fraud in connection with the provision of the service requested", or "to enable … automatically authenticating the identity of the subscriber or user". The ICO's final SAT guidance (29 Apr 2026) confirms "preventing or detecting fraud" and "authenticating the subscriber or user" as qualifying activities.
- **The condition:** "if you say your use of a particular technology is strictly necessary because of the purpose (eg security), you **must** ensure that you only use it for this purpose. If you use it for any other purpose as well, the exception does not apply and you must then get consent." (ICO SAT guidance.)
- **Definitive:** no consent, no interstitial, for a security-only same-site fingerprint. UK GDPR side: Art 6(1)(f) + LIA (check DUAA Sch 4 recognised legitimate interests — unverified in R2). Children's design duties (DUAA s.81 + AADC) attach separately (§8).
- **Minimum:** policy + addendum + LIA + DPIA + children's handling. If any analytics/A-B/personalisation event ever keys off the identifier, the exception is gone and consent becomes mandatory for the whole thing.

### (c) US (California as the operative state law) — **no consent, notice only**

- A device fingerprint is a "unique identifier … persistent or probabilistic identifier" → **PI, not SPI** (Cal. Civ. Code §1798.140(aj)); precise geolocation (§1798.140(ae)(1)(C)) and religious belief (§1798.140(ae)(1)(D)) are SPI — neither is collected by the design. Security/fraud is a "business purpose" (§1798.140(e)(2); §1798.140(ac)), and §7050(a)(4) lets a service provider process PI to detect security incidents/fraud.
- **GPC:** §7025(b) binds "a business that sells or shares personal information". Security-only, no vendor pooling → not triggered (do not build an opt-out link for this; do not let a vendor pool).
- **Notice at collection:** required only if the app is a "business" under §1798.140(d)(1) thresholds — a small non-commercial app with no selling/sharing is almost certainly not (R2 §5.1), **the repo mounts `/billing/v1` with subscription/invoice/payment models, but it is non-functional (8 of 9 tables unmigrated, E3) — re-verify commercial status if billing is ever revived.** If it is a business: notice at collection (§1798.100(a); CPPA §7012) including the retention period for each category — the policy table satisfies this. ADMT does **not** apply: §7200 reaches only "significant decisions" and §7001 defines those as financial/lending, housing, education, employment, healthcare — a Quran-app ban is not one.
- **COPPA (<13):** persistent identifiers are PI when used to recognise a user over time; the **"support for internal operations"** exception (16 CFR 312.2) covers security/fraud prevention/protecting the security or integrity of the user/service, provided no behavioural advertising and no third-party disclosure beyond support. The 2025 amendments add biometric identifiers to PI and cap retention at "as long as reasonably necessary" — the 30d/12mo ladder is defensible under that standard if disclosed in the children's notice.
- **Minimum:** policy disclosure (what, why, retention, no sale/share) + the addendum; **no interstitial.** If under-13s can use the service, a COPPA-compliant children's notice.

### (d) India — **consent required (or do not process)**

- DPDP has **no legitimate-interest basis** and no ePrivacy-style carve-out (R2 §6.3). Processing rests on consent (s.6) or s.7 "certain legitimate uses" — and R2 could not verify whether fraud prevention is in s.7 (uncertainty register #1: "the item most likely to force a design change").
- **Definitive:** for Indian users, either (i) **do not process device data** (recommended: a no-device mode; the reader stays fully usable; rate limiting stays IP-based), or (ii) consent-gated with a **standalone, itemised Rule 3 notice** (plain language, itemised description of personal data + specified purposes, how to withdraw consent and exercise rights), free/specific/informed/unconditional consent with clear affirmative action, withdrawal as easy as giving, and retention aligned with Rule 8. Substantive Rules 3, 5–16 commence **13 May 2027** — build now, not then.
- Under-18s: no device processing (known constraint; design change in §8). Rule 10 verifiable parental consent is not satisfiable by self-declaration, so the honest posture is no account-level device records for self-declared minors.
- **Minimum:** a jurisdiction gate that disables the collector for India by default, or a consent panel that is a Rule 3 notice (not a cookie banner). No banner for anyone else.

### (e) Summary verdict on the design's "no consent UI"

Correct for **UK + US**; incorrect for **EU if the client probes stay**; incorrect for **India**. The design needs a jurisdiction-aware gate, not a global banner, and the EU decision is really a collection-architecture decision (server-side-only vs client probes).

## 4. Audit of the "no behavioral biometrics" claim

**The claim is correct today, with three caveats and one enforcement requirement.**

- **The legal test.** GDPR Art 4(14): biometric data is "personal data resulting from specific technical processing relating to the physical, physiological or **behavioural** characteristics of a natural person". AI Act Art 3(34) is identical; Art 3(35) remote biometric identification requires establishing identity by comparing biometric data. A device/browser fingerprint derives from device and browser configuration, not from a person's characteristics. The standard reading: **not biometric, not RBI** (R2 §7.3). AI Act Annex III(1)(a) is therefore not engaged; Annex III high-risk obligations are anyway deferred to 2 Dec 2027 (mirror caveat), Art 2(7) preserves GDPR/ePrivacy in full, and Art 2(12) open-source release is an additional shield — not a substitute for GDPR compliance.
- **Where the line is.** The line is not "how many signals" — it is **whether the processing characterises the person's behaviour**. Device configuration, coherence checks, sighting counters, and trust state stay on the device side. The moment any signal is derived from **how the person interacts** — keystroke timing, mouse/touch dynamics, scroll cadence, reading pace, tap pressure, gaze, voice/recitation — it becomes a behavioural characteristic of a person; if it is then used to identify, it is Art 9 biometric-for-unique-identification territory and Annex III(1)(a) RBI (when applicable). **This is a live risk in this repo:** the app is a Quran app with recitation audio, and `docs/remaining/feature-gap-catalogue.md` lists "voice input" for search as a remaining feature. A voice-input feature analysed for identification, or recitation analysis, would cross the line.
- **Caveat 1 — `WASM/JS timing signature`.** A fixed compute benchmark is a device-class signal, but "timing signature" measured through input handling is behavioural. Pin it: benchmark executed in a fixed harness with no input-event timing; prefer dropping it or keeping only a coarse class (R2 §8.4 lists it in the high-entropy set to avoid).
- **Caveat 2 — trust scoring is profiling even when it is not biometric.** Art 4(4) profiling: trust_state + risk score evaluate personal aspects (reliability/abuse propensity) of the person behind the device. That does not make it biometric, but it does make Art 13(2)(f)/Art 22/DPIA/AADC analysis apply. Do not use the "not biometric" conclusion to imply "not profiling".
- **Caveat 3 — the collector payload is the real control point.** Add a **payload schema allowlist** (a typed struct that cannot carry input-dynamics fields) + a test that fails if new keys appear. Prose prohibitions in Layer 0 are not enforcement.
- **Verdict:** outside biometrics as designed; **do not describe it as "biometric identification"** (wrong and risky, R2 §7.3); add the schema allowlist + a standing prohibition on voice/recitation/input-dynamics signals; treat the trust score as profiling for transparency and DPIA purposes.

## 5. DSAR / erasure — can a user delete their device records, and how?

**Today: no.** There is no API, no admin tool, no policy statement, and the design's `device_key` is HMAC-derived (not reversible). But erasure does not require reversal — it requires acting on the _stored record_, and the schema supports lookup by `user_id`. The missing pieces are the token→record link, the endpoints, the procedure, and the policy sentence.

### 5.1 The legal position

- **Access (Art 15(1))**: the user can ask what device keys are linked to them, why, since when, what was decided. The `device_records.user_id` index + `device_sessions` support this. Answer within one month (Art 12(3)).
- **Erasure (Art 17)**: applies. **Exception:** an active ban record may be retained/restricted under **Art 17(3)(e)** (defence of legal claims) and **Art 6(1)(f)**; this is a _restriction_, not free retention — strip everything not needed to enforce the ban and defend it.
- **Objection (Art 21(1))**: the user may object to LI processing; for security you may refuse on compelling legitimate grounds, but you must have the process and document the assessment.
- **India** s.12(3) (erasure) + Rule 8; **LGPD** Art 18 (deletion); **CCPA** §1798.105 (delete) if a business. Same machinery answers all four.

### 5.2 Operational procedure (draft — DSAR-DEV v1)

1. **Intake.** (a) Authenticated self-service: Settings → "Security & devices" → **"Delete my device data"** (immediate, no ticket). (b) Email via `/contact` for users with no account. Every request gets an ID and a timestamp.
2. **Verification.** Account-linked request: the authenticated session is sufficient. Device-only request: accept the **first-party device token** the browser holds as a weak authenticator for _that device's own_ record only — never for records of other devices/users. No token, no account → you cannot verify; respond explaining that the device-only record is pseudonymous and not searchable from natural identifiers (R2 §8.6), and tell the user how to clear the local token.
3. **Classification.** For the verified subject: (i) no active ban → delete the `device_records` row(s) and the `device_sessions` links; (ii) active ban → restricted processing: keep `device_key` + reason class + ban window + expiry + decision ref; **delete** `trust_state`, `seen_count`, `coherence booleans`, `user_id` link (SET NULL already), and any evidence beyond the hold minimum.
4. **Execute + log.** Every request writes an append-only audit row: request ID, verification method, action, fields removed, fields retained + the Art 17(3)(e) reason, response date. This is the Art 5(2) evidence and the answer to a DPA.
5. **Respond** within one month (Art 12(3)), stating: what was deleted; what was kept and why (active ban + legal-claims restriction); when the kept record expires; and the honest limitation — **because `device_key` is a deterministic HMAC of the same signals, continued use of the service will create a new record under the same purpose**. Do not pretend erasure is permanent against future use; disclose it.
6. **Backups.** Deletion propagates within the documented backup rotation window; expired/deleted keys are not restored into the active store (R2 §8.5). State the window in the SOP and the policy.
7. **Escalation lever.** Global pepper rotation is the only "forget every device" action; it invalidates all bans keyed under the old pepper. It is an incident-response tool, not a per-user tool. Document that in the SOP so nobody promises it.
8. **What must be added to the design:** (a) a `device_token_hash` column (or mapping table) so the server can find a record from the client-held token; (b) `DELETE /api/account/device-records` (authenticated) and a client "Forget this device" action that clears localStorage/IDB + calls it; (c) `POST /admin/devices/{id}/erase` with reason + audit row for emailed requests; (d) the policy statement ("How to ask us to delete device data; what we must keep if you are banned and why; how long").

## 6. Automated decision-making — Art 22 audit of L0–L7

**Applicability caveat:** for a free Quran reader, "similarly significant" is arguable (R2 §2.2 — no credit/employment/housing/essential-service denial). But three things make the strict design cheap and the loose design expensive: (i) the Dutch AP's Uber decision treated **temporary fraud-suspicion deactivations** as automated decisions with zero human intervention (€824,990,000, Art 22 + Art 13); (ii) LGPD Art 20 gives a **review right without any "significant effect" requirement** — a ban imposed by automation is likely within it; (iii) DSA Art 20(6) requires platform decisions to be taken "under the supervision of appropriately qualified staff, and not solely on the basis of automated means" (subject to the micro/small exemption, §9). Design to the strictest of the three.

### 6.1 Rung-by-rung

| Rung                                               | Is it a "decision"?                                                                    | Verdict                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| L0 observe, L1 throttle, L2 challenge, L3 cooldown | No — proportionate, reversible, act on the abusive _action_, not the person (R2 §7.1). | Automated OK.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **L4 hold for review (≤72h)**                      | Only if it restricts the user.                                                         | **Ambiguity is a defect.** Define L4 as a back-office queue with **no user-visible restriction**; if the account/device is frozen during the hold, it is L5 and needs a human.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **L5 suspend**                                     | **Yes** — a service restriction with real impact.                                      | **Not defensible as an automatic rung.** Human approval required, or: automatic interim suspension with hard expiry ≤72h, a durable notice, an immediate contest path, and **auto-reversal if no human confirms**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **L6 ban + second look**                           | **Yes.**                                                                               | **"Second look" after the ban takes effect is not human intervention in the decision process.** The AP's words: "l'absence totale d'intervention humaine dans le processus de décision". A post-hoc review where the user must appeal is the Uber pattern with extra steps. Required: the ban takes effect only after a human confirms; if urgency demands immediate action, execute a temporary measure (see L5) and let the human confirm or reverse. Permanent ban: two-person rule (proposer + confirmer) where a second reviewer exists; with one operator, auto-expiring bans renewed by a recorded self-review (B9 F4). |
| L7 legal referral                                  | Human by definition.                                                                   | OK; document the referral log.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

### 6.2 What the design must record to prove human involvement

Per L5/L6 decision, a durable record with: `decision_id`, `rule_version`, `score`, `reason_codes[]`, `prior_state`, `new_state`, `automated_proposal` (bool), `reviewer_id`, `reviewed_at`, **`rationale` (required, non-empty free text — the single field that proves a human actually thought)**, `second_reviewer_id` (permanent bans), `notice_delivery` (channel, timestamp, proof), `appeal_id`, `appeal_reviewer_id` (must differ from the first reviewer where a second reviewer exists), `appeal_submitted_at`, `appeal_outcome`, `appeal_decided_at`. Plus Art 13(2)(f)/15(1)(h) user-facing information (logic, significance, consequences) in the ban notice — the Uber fine included Art 13, not just Art 22.

### 6.3 Durable notice — a delivery gap

A ban that revokes the session means the user cannot log in to read the notice. "Durable notice" must mean: (a) an email to the account address (if known) containing the statement of reasons; and (b) a page reachable **without authentication** (e.g., a signed ban-reference link) that shows the decision, the reason code in plain language, the human-review fact, the appeal route, and the retention. If neither channel exists (device-only ban, no account, no email), record that no notice was deliverable and why — DSA Art 17 is conditioned on "where the relevant electronic contact details are known".

## 7. Transparency — the policy paragraph

### 7.1 The paragraph the repo needs (device identification)

> To keep accounts secure and stop abuse, we build a device identifier from a few technical signals your browser and connection send, hashed with a secret key we store separately. We use it only for account security and abuse prevention, never for advertising or analytics, never to profile what you read, and never sold or shared. We do not infer or use your religious beliefs, and this record is not linked to your reading, bookmarks or search. The identifier expires after 30 days; a banned device keeps a minimal record for 12 months, and every ban is reviewed by a person and can be appealed.

Four sentences, no legalese padding. It sits in the policy's device section **and** in the one-page "Device security and abuse prevention" addendum, which also carries the legal-basis line, the retention table, the rights, and the Turnstile/Cloudflare mention.

### 7.2 What else the policy must carry (Art 13/14 + Rule 3 + §7012)

Controller identity and contact; purposes and legal basis **per purpose** (including "legitimate interests: security and abuse prevention" with a link to the LIA summary); retention per category (device key 30d, ban record 12mo, push token, logs, analytics); recipients (Cloudflare, Google/Firebase, any mail provider); international transfers + mechanism; rights (access, rectification, erasure, objection, portability, withdraw consent where consent is the basis, complain to a DPA); automated decision-making information (human review, appeal, logic summary) if Art 22 applies; the "we do not infer religious belief" sentence; the India itemised notice (Rule 3) in the India-facing variant. **The current policy is a placeholder marked "not legal advice" — this is a rewrite, not a patch.** Distinguish the new `device_key` from the existing push token ("we store a push token so notifications reach your device") or users will conflate them.

## 8. Minors

**The design says nothing about minors. That is the gap, and the safest change is cheap.**

- **GDPR Art 8** only gates consent-based ISS processing — the design uses Art 6(1)(f), so parental consent is not directly required. But Art 6(1)(f) itself is weakened "in particular where the data subject is a child", Recital 38 requires specific protection, the EDPB Guidelines 1/2024 draft has a dedicated children's chapter, and children are a WP248 DPIA criterion. So: child-specific balancing + DPIA, not Art 8 parental consent, for the EU.
- **UK:** DUAA s.81 inserts a children's higher-protection duty into data protection by design; the AADC applies if the service is likely to be accessed by children. AADC standard on profiling: trust-scoring a child's device, linked to their account, is profiling of a child — minimise or exclude.
- **US (COPPA):** under-13 persistent identifiers are PI; the "support for internal operations" exception (16 CFR 312.2) covers security/fraud/protecting the service, provided no behavioural advertising and no third-party disclosure beyond support. The 2025 amendments add biometric identifiers to PI and cap retention at "as long as reasonably necessary" — the 30d/12mo ladder fits if disclosed in a children's notice and not extended.
- **India:** no device processing for under-18s (known constraint; Rule 10 verifiable parental consent is not satisfiable by self-declaration).
- **Safest design change (recommended):**
  1. **Self-declared date of birth at signup** → a `minor` flag. Do not build age _estimation_ or biometric age assurance (that is itself profiling/biometric-adjacent).
  2. **When `minor` is set: the device collector is disabled for that account entirely, and any existing device records are deleted.** Push-token retention for minors follows the same schedule (the existing `devices` table has no expiry — add one).
  3. **No fingerprint for logged-out readers** — the design attaches only to `AuthClient.#request`, but that path also carries the anonymous CSRF bootstrap and the session probe (A4 §7), so an explicit path allowlist (never `/csrf/v1/generate`, never `/user/v1/get`) is what makes this true; convert it into a machine-guarded test (the collector module must not be importable from reader routes).
  4. **No accounts for self-declared under-18s in India** (or a fully anonymous, no-device kids mode) — the only clean answer.
  5. Document the honest limitation: self-declaration is not verification; you have made a reasonable-effort design choice and minimised the data, which is the proportionate posture for a free reader.
  6. For under-13s in the US: a COPPA children's notice naming the persistent identifier, the internal-operations purpose, the retention, and the no-disclosure rule.

## 9. DSA — the design's ban ladder against Arts 17/19/20

**Repo fact that changes the analysis: the service already hosts user content.** `/post/v1` and `/post/comment/v1` are mounted unconditionally; posts appear in `/sitemap.xml` and in RSS/Atom (`feed_v1`); `comment_flag` and admin hide/delete/flag endpoints exist. So the service is a **hosting service**, and (because posts are publicly disseminated) plausibly an **online platform** — this is not a "if you add comments later" question. R2's conditional ("a read-only reader owes neither") does not apply to this repo as it stands.

- **Art 17 (Statement of reasons)** — sits in Section 2 and is **not exempted** by Art 19's micro/small exclusion (R2 §7.2; structural reading, flagged as analysis). It applies to "suspension or termination of the service in whole or in part" and "suspension or termination of the recipient's account", **"regardless of why or how it was imposed"**, from the date the restriction is imposed, where contact details are known. It must state: the decision and its scope/duration; the facts and circumstances relied on; **where applicable, the use made of automated means**; the contractual ground and why; and clear, user-friendly redress information.
  - **The design's ban ladder therefore triggers Art 17 at L5 (suspend) and L6 (ban) whenever the restricted user has an account/email.** "Appeal flow with durable notice" is close but must be checked against the Art 17 content list, the timing rule, and the automated-means disclosure.
  - **Existing exposure, independent of this design:** the comment moderation endpoints (admin hide/delete) already restrict user content; if those are used, Art 17 statements of reasons may already be owed today. Surface this to the maintainers.
  - Device-only bans with no account and no email: no contact details known → no Art 17 duty; record that.
- **Art 20 (Internal complaint-handling)** — applies to online platforms, requires a free electronic complaint system for at least **six months** after the decision, and **Art 20(6)** requires decisions to be taken under the supervision of qualified staff, not solely automated. **But Art 19 exempts micro/small online platforms from Section 3 (Arts 20–28).** A small non-commercial project qualifies; the exemption is lost on growth (Recommendation 2003/361/EC: <50 staff and ≤€10m turnover for "small"). **Design implication:** build the appeal flow anyway (it is required by Art 22(3) for L5/L6 and by LGPD Art 20), model it on Art 20 (6-month window, reversal without undue delay, qualified staff), and note the exemption status as time-limited. The `/billing/v1` module means turnover must be re-checked before relying on micro/small.
- **Also relevant:** EDPB Guidelines 3/2025 on the DSA/GDPR interplay (final 17 Sept 2026) for how the ban notice and the GDPR transparency duties interact; DSA Art 14 (terms) should name abuse bans as a contractual ground so Art 17 has a ground to cite.
- **Required changes:** (a) a DSA hosting-service re-assessment note is unnecessary — the service is already in scope; instead, an **Art 17 statement-of-reasons template** (with facts, automated-means flag, redress) and a rule that L5/L6 notices use it; (b) appeal SOP with the Art 20 shape; (c) a review trigger for when the project ceases to be micro/small; (d) disclosure of the comment/content moderation Art 17 gap that exists today.

## 11. Findings (max 15)

Each finding is new relative to the "already known" list.

### F1 — Client-side probe set has no consent path and no per-Member-State ePrivacy analysis

**Severity: HIGH. Hook: ePrivacy Art 5(3); EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2.0 ¶2/¶4; Planet49 C-673/17 ¶70; ICO SAT guidance 29 Apr 2026.**
The L1 collector reads WebGL/WebGPU renderer + extension hash + shader precision, WASM/JS timing, UA-CH high-entropy values, and writes a token to localStorage + IDB. That is terminal access + storage. The EDPB refuses to interpret the strictly-necessary exemption (¶4: case-by-case, per member-state transposition), so a high-entropy client probe set cannot be assumed exempt EU-wide. **Change:** either move collection server-side (headers/TLS only — the LINC/CNIL-approved lower-intrusion point, R2 §8.4), or keep the probes and add just-in-time consent at login/signup/report with equal-weight one-click decline, never gating the reader. Write the per-MS analysis either way. **NEW.**

### F2 — `device_records` has no `expires_at`; the retention ladder is unenforceable

**Severity: HIGH. Hook: GDPR Art 5(1)(e), Art 5(2); CNIL retention doctrine ("document your choice"); India Rule 8(3).**
m000012 defines id, device_key, user_id, first_seen, last_seen, seen_count, trust_state, coherence booleans, evidence JSON, created_at — and no expiry column, no deletion job, no backup propagation. A 30d/12mo ladder in a bullet is not a retention mechanism. **Change:** `expires_at NOT NULL` + CHECK on every device table; scheduled deletion job with logged count; documented backup rotation window and no-restore rule. **NEW.**

### F3 — Stored `trust_state` + `seen_count` + coherence booleans are a device profile; they leak past the key's expiry

**Severity: HIGH. Hook: GDPR Art 4(4) (profiling), Recital 30 (online identifiers + device factors), Art 5(1)(e); LGPD Art 12 §2; CCPA §1798.140(aj).**
Answer to the direct question: **yes, in effect** — the row is pseudonymous personal data keyed by a fingerprint-derived key, and trust_state/seen_count/coherence booleans add a behavioural profile of the device. Not biometric, but profiling. As written, nothing expires them; they would survive into the ban record and contradict the "ban record = key + reason + date + reviewer" claim. **Change:** field-level expiry — on key expiry delete the row or reduce to count-only with no key and no profile fields; define `evidence JSON` as decision metadata only (never raw signals/content/IP); add the module-import guard + payload allowlist because `analytics_v1`/`search_v1`/`post_view` exist and an accidental join to content is a live risk. **NEW.**

### F4 — L5 suspend / L6 ban automation matches the Uber pattern; a post-hoc "second look" is not Art 22 human intervention

**Severity: CRITICAL. Hook: GDPR Art 22(1)+(3), Art 13(2)(f); WP251rev.01; Dutch AP Uber €824,990,000 (Art 22 + Art 13; temporary fraud deactivations with "l'absence totale d'intervention humaine dans le processus de décision", 24 Aug 2026, under appeal); LGPD Art 20 (broader review right); DSA Art 20(6).**
"L5 suspend" and "L6 ban + second look" read as automated rungs; the AP's facts include _temporary_ suspensions on suspicion. **Change:** nothing above L3 automatic; L4 = no user-visible restriction (or it is L5); L5 = human-approved or ≤72h auto-expiring interim with auto-reversal and immediate contest; L6 = human decision before effect; permanent bans need a recorded second look (two-person where possible, otherwise auto-expiry + recorded self-review); record reviewer_id, non-empty rationale, rule version, score, reason codes, state transition, appeal reviewer + outcome, notice proof. **NEW.**

### F5 — "No consent UI" is correct for the UK and US, wrong for the EU client probes and India

**Severity: HIGH. Hook: ePrivacy Art 5(3); PECR Sch A1 ¶4(2)(b)–(e) (in force 5 Feb 2026) + ICO SAT sole-purpose rule; CCPA §1798.100/§7012 (notice only, PI not SPI, GPC not triggered absent sale/share); DPDP Act s.6 + Rules 3/8 (consent + itemised notice).**
Definitive per-jurisdiction: EU — consent required for the client-probe set as designed, not required if collection moves server-side; UK — no consent, including probes; US — no consent/interstitial, notice at collection only if a "business" (`/billing/v1` is mounted but non-functional, E3); India — consent required or do not process. **Change:** a jurisdiction-aware gate (no global banner), a no-device mode for India, and the just-in-time EU panel only if client probes stay. **NEW.**

### F6 — `request.cf` fields in signed edge headers risk injecting precise geolocation

**Severity: HIGH. Hook: GDPR Art 5(1)(c); CCPA §1798.140(ae)(1)(C) precise geolocation SPI (≤1,850 ft) + §1798.140(w); FTC sensitive-location enforcement priority.**
Layer 4 says the Worker adds signed `X-Eq-Edge-*` headers from "request.cf fields" — that object includes city, latitude, longitude, postalCode, timezone, region. **Change:** explicit allowlist (country, ASN, TLS/HTTP protocol metadata only); never city/lat/long/postal/timezone; never persist any edge field with the device key; document Cloudflare's processor role, DPA and transfer basis. **NEW.**

### F7 — DSAR/erasure is not executable; the ban carve-out needs a per-matter legal hold, not a category rule

**Severity: HIGH. Hook: GDPR Art 15, Art 17(3)(e), Art 12(3), Art 21(1); DPDP s.12(3); LGPD Art 18.**
There is no API, no admin erase tool, no policy statement, and no token→record link. HMAC non-reversibility is not an obstacle to erasure (erasure acts on the stored row, looked up by user_id) but it _is_ an obstacle to per-user permanence, which must be disclosed. **Change:** implement §5.2 (self-service delete + admin erase endpoint + audit rows + one-month response + backup propagation + honest "continued use recreates the record" disclosure); keep a minimal ban record under a per-matter hold (reason, authority ref, owner, expiry) and delete everything else. **NEW.**

### F8 — 30-day key vs India's Rule 8(3) one-year retention floor

**Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH. Hook: DPDP Rules 2025 Rule 8(3) (minimum 1 year for personal data + traffic/logs for prescribed purposes); Rule 3 notice.**
